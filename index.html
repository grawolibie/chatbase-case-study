Task 1: Target

The question I used to guide everything: where does an AE add value that the product can't deliver on its own? Chatbase is built to be self-served, so if I can't answer that, I'm chasing customers who would sign up anyway.

This site covers how I defined the ICP and chose my 10 accounts, plus a brief for each account. My outreach sequence and first-call plan for my #1 account are in Tasks 2 and 3, further down.

1. Understanding the problem

Takeaway: Chatbase's own customers showed me what the product solves, but not who needs an AE.

I started with Chatbase's published customer stories, because I didn't want to guess what a good customer looks like. I used Perplexity to group them by use case. Three were verifiable Shopify merchants (West Coast Batteries, LUV Health and Rocksteady), so I focused on those.

The use cases fell into three groups:

  • Helping shoppers buy: matching batteries to vehicles (West Coast Batteries), answering questions before purchase and capturing leads (LUV Health).
  • Cutting repetitive support: answering common questions 24/7 and redirecting email to the agent (Rocksteady).
  • Saving staff time: staff using the agent to find product answers instead of searching several systems (West Coast Batteries).

These three groups became my first pillar: does this merchant have a problem Chatbase can solve that's worth real money?

But all three merchants set Chatbase up on their own. So these stories told me what Chatbase can do, not which merchants need a salesperson. That became the question for the rest of my work.

2. Defining who to target

Takeaway: A merchant is worth an AE's time only if Chatbase can help, the deal is winnable, and self-serve isn't enough.

Pillar 2: will they actually buy?

While looking for signals, I realized product fit only covers one side. A merchant can have the right problem and still not buy, because of an existing tool, no budget, or no reason to act now. So signals also had to come from the merchant's situation, not just the product. I added five questions:

  • Do they recognize the problem?
  • Is there a reason to act now? (weighted highest, because timing drives deals)
  • Is there a real opening against what they use today?
  • Do they have budget and an owner?
  • Could they actually adopt it?

Pillar 3: does this deal need an AE?

This is the question the role is really about. Chatbase applies to many kinds of problems, and from my own experience as an AE, that flexibility is where a salesperson adds value: shaping a solution the self-serve setup can't. That's true for merchants with several stores, brands or markets, security and governance needs, or integrations beyond Chatbase's built-in Shopify actions.

Chatbase's pricing made this concrete. Self-serve plans top out at 5 seats and 15,000 message credits a month, and features like single sign-on, audit logs and agent-level permissions are Enterprise-only. A merchant who needs those is, by definition, an AE account. Because this is the point of the role, I made this pillar pass/fail instead of a score.

Where the obvious answer is wrong

Before relying on these pillars, I asked: what would make a merchant look like a great fit on paper but not be one? I checked Chatbase's documentation and pricing, what competitors can do, and how Chatbase's customers actually deployed. Four things came up:

  1. Size and volume don't predict value. What matters is how much can actually be automated. Shopify already handles order tracking, and a big catalog can be easy to search. So I dropped catalog size as a signal and gave order-status questions little weight.
  2. Chatbase has real limits. It handles returns, exchanges and replacements, but can't cancel orders or change addresses, which Gorgias can. Custom workflows need scoping, which is AE work. Merchants whose support is mostly cancellations went into my anti-ICP.
  3. A good fit can still lose the deal. Competitors, message-credit costs, revenue that's hard to prove, and unclear budget owners can all stop a purchase. Working through those is what an AE does, and it's why Pillar 2 matters.
  4. Many customers don't need an AE at all. Chatbase's own customers went live on their own in an hour to a week. So an AE should spend time only where self-serve falls short, which is why Pillar 3 is a gate.
How I verified this: I checked my early assumptions against Chatbase's Shopify documentation, and two were out of date. Exchanges on the original order now run without approval, and missing or damaged items can be replaced automatically. I corrected both before building on them.

My ICP

Merchants whose customers need help buying or getting support, whose current tools don't provide it, and who are too complex to set it up alone. One part per pillar.

Full version: Shopify merchants whose shoppers need guidance to buy (real selection, compatibility or fit decisions), or who handle high volumes of post-purchase questions Chatbase can resolve (returns, exchanges, replacements, product care), whose current stack leaves that gap open, and whose deployment needs more than self-serve can deliver.

Who I'd skip (anti-ICP):

  • Simple catalogs where support is mostly "where's my order." Shopify already covers that.
  • Support dominated by cancellations and address changes. Chatbase can't do these natively; Gorgias can.
  • Single-store merchants with a simple catalog. Self-serve handles them well.
  • Merchants already running a well-deployed AI agent with no visible gap.

Scoring rubric

Every criterion is scored 0 to 5 against the same evidence standard:

0Confirmed bad fitVerified absence or a clear disqualifier

2PlausibleRelevant workflow found, but not yet shown to matter
3CredibleSpecific evidence and a defensible hypothesis
4StrongSubstantial evidence or a concrete initiative
5ValidatedDirect confirmation

Three rules I applied:

  • Missing evidence scores 1, so gaps in my research can't make an account look better.
  • Pillar 3 is a gate: an account only qualifies if at least one AE criterion scores 3 or higher.
  • Ties go to the account I can actually reach: a named sponsor, a workable time zone, a shared language.

4. From framework to 10 accounts

Takeaway: I narrowed the list one pillar at a time, scored what was left honestly, and checked every live site before finalizing.

I researched in three passes, one per pillar:

  1. Is there a real problem? I started here because without a reason to use Chatbase, nothing else matters.
  2. Could we win it? A merchant that could benefit isn't a deal unless there's an opening and a reason to act.
  3. Would an AE add value? Last, because a merchant can be a good customer without needing an AE.
